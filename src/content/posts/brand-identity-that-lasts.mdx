Brand identity is more than a logo. It's the sum of every interaction a customer has with your business — from your website to your packaging to how your team answers the phone. Getting it right requires both creative intuition and strategic clarity.

## Start with Strategy, Not Design

Before you open a design tool, answer these questions:

- **Who is our target customer?** Be specific.
- **What is our core promise?** One sentence.
- **What are our values?** Three to five, max.
- **What's our brand personality?** (e.g., confident and direct vs. warm and approachable)

## The Core Identity System

A durable brand identity typically includes:

1. **Logo** — Primary, secondary, and icon variants
2. **Color palette** — Primary, secondary, neutral, and functional colors
3. **Typography** — Display and body typefaces with clear hierarchy rules
4. **Photography style** — Art direction guidelines for imagery
5. **Voice and tone** — How you write and speak

## Consistency Is the Brand

A brand is only as strong as its consistency. A world-class logo used inconsistently is less effective than a mediocre one applied uniformly. Invest in a brand guidelines document — even a single-page PDF — and enforce it.

## Evolve, Don't Reinvent

Strong brands evolve gradually. Minor refinements every few years (tightening typography, modernizing color) keep a brand fresh without losing equity. Complete rebrands are expensive and risky — reserved for genuine strategic repositioning.
